Character Arcs

Your antagonist should believe they're right

The antagonist in your story must beleive in the cause they're fighting for. Whether their actions are inherently evil or not, they must beleive they're justified in their actions.

Make them capable

An antagonist should be a direct threat to the protagonist. They should be as capable or more, but never weaker than the protagonist. This must be the case regardless of the ability the story needs.

Connect to the main themes

The antagonist must make the protagonist struggle in an aspect of themselves they must overcome. A good antagonist invites fear, doubt, failure, and exposure of the protagonists weaknesses and insecurities.

Character Arc Types

There are three main types of character arcs, choose the one that best fits your character!

Character Arc Type Description Recommendation
Positive Arc A character that learns and grows as a result of their experiences Great for protagonists or supporting characters that need to learn something throughout the story
Negative Arc A character beleives a lie that causes them to regress or become worse Suitable for antagonists or characters that serve as warnings
Flat Arc A character who remains unchanged in their beliefs but change the conflict and characters around them Ideal for protagonists of long running series or characters that don't need any development
